[Architecture decisions](../03-architecture/architecture-decisions.md) (AD-xx) summarise the technical ones and link back by ID; when the two disagree, this log wins.

Rules:

- IDs were assigned on 2026-10-12, when this log was compiled from the team's notes and plans. Dates are the day the choice was recorded (2026). Check each row against your own notes and correct it if the team remembers it differently.

| D-56 | Oct 19 | The O-13 values stay as built; the team agreed them. Each has a recorded reason: (1) **Poverty bands 10/20/30%** are a display grouping only; no flag or score uses them (the socioeconomic flag is the highest fifth). Round cutoffs keep the legend readable. (2) **Gap fifths**: BQ1 asks where the gaps are largest, which is a relative question; quintiles are the usual grouping in equity analysis. Labels say "lowest 20% of planning units", never "inadequate", because the data has no national planning norm to compare with. (3) **"Two of three" workforce rule**: a workforce gap is flagged only when most of the three core professions (doctors, nurses, midwives) are in the lowest fifth, so one profession's reporting gap cannot raise it alone. The three are the WHO health-worker density measure behind SDG 3.c.1; its 44.5 per 10,000 threshold is not applied because our counts are public sector only. (4) **Three weight sets**: `equal` is the default because no gap type has a basis to count more; `supply_heavy` and `equity_heavy` are sensitivity tests, and the claim rests on the units that stay at the top under all three. (5) **80% dialysis station completeness**: a data-quality gate, so stations per 100,000 is not shown when more than a fifth of a unit's dialysis facilities have no station count. Evidence for (2)–(5) comes from `gold_o13_evidence.sql` in the gold pass. This closes O-13. | Every value is a judgement, not a published standard. A reason and evidence per value make the choice defensible and show the answer does not hinge on it. Options to strengthen later, for team decision: poverty bands relative to the national incidence of the same year, and the combined doctor-nurse-midwife density in place of "two of three" if Q5 shows low agreement. |

Both are dropped for now because no silver domain is near a million rows, and both move to the recommendations. Full explanation: [rebuild strategy](../04-pipeline/silver/rebuild-strategy.md). Replaces the "per-source rebuild next" plan of 2026-10-16 (AD-15). | The waste is rebuilding unchanged inputs, not row volume: sources change rarely and bronze already skips unchanged files. At about 565k diseases rows (checked 2026-10-16) a full rebuild is cheap, every gate checks the whole table, and a rule change such as D-44 reaches every row at once. Incremental builds add per-source and cross-source gates and an equality test for a moderate saving at this volume. Under a million rows, `OPTIMIZE` and Z-order cost more compute than they save, and partitioning small tables creates many small files. |
